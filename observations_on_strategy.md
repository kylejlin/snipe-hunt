# Snipe Hunt Observations on Strategy

Snipe Hunt is a new game, and the theory has not been thoroughly studied.
However, after playing a few games, I've coined these terms:

- _Material_ matters. Major pieces are more valuable than minor ones.
- _Pressure_ matters. When you can capture a rank, we say you are _pressing_ that rank.
  - Obviously, if you press all the ranks, and it's your turn, then you win (since you can capture the enemy snipe).
  - If you press a rank, but the opponent does not, we say that you _control_ that rank. Control is often very valuable.
- A _sanctuary_ rank is one that your opponent is not currently pressing.
  - Obviously, you need to keep at least one sanctuary (for your snipe). Often, this is one of the ranks on your side of the field (since you have the advantage of your opponent being unable to drop retreaters there).
  - One strategy to protect a sanctuary is to place enough animals there to press the neighboring ranks. (We call this a _trench_). That way, if enemies start amassing on the neighboring ranks, your animals in the trench can capture them before they amass enough to pose a threat to the trench. Of course, this is much less effective if the opponent can simply drop retreaters right into (or behind) your trench, which is why the "home side" of the field is usually easier to defend.
- If you can get retreaters to "break through" your opponent's main line(s), this is often, but not always, helpful. This can be difficult (especially in the opening), since you can't drop retreaters into the far two ranks, which means the only way to infiltrate is to advance. But if you advance, normally entrenched defenders can capture you before you can do damage.

Do NOT treat these concepts as authoritative facts; treat them as disprovable **hypotheses**.
Some or all may turn out to be false with more research.
